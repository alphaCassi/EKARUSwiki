Copernico telescope

The Copernico 1.82 m on Mount Ekar (Asiago) is the largest optical telescope in Italy. It is operated by INAF – Astronomical Observatory of Padova and has been in use since 1973 for imaging, low- to medium-high resolution spectroscopy and polarimetry.

Its two regular instruments, AFOSC (spectrograph and camera) and the Echelle high-resolution spectrograph, are mounted in turn following the Moon cycle; AFOSC usually takes grey and dark time. EKARUS sits at the Coudé focus.

Source: INAF–OAPd, Copernico 1.82 m

Site and telescope

LocationMt. Ekar, Asiago (INAF – Asiago Astrophysical Observatory)
CoordinatesLat. 45° 50′ 54.894″ N · Long. 11° 34′ 08.397″ E · Alt. 1376.2 m
Optical designClassic Cassegrain, f/9
Primary mirror (M1)182 cm, Schott Duran 50; re-aluminized periodically in the vacuum chamber at Mt. Ekar
Secondary mirror (M2)Moves in focus (Z axis) only
Cassegrain focusf/9, 12.6″/mm, about 20 cm from the rear flange; main focus
Nasmyth focus45° tertiary mirror, East side of the fork
Coudé focusHosts EKARUS; F/20.5 beam to the bench. Relay upgraded April 2026 with new L1 and L3 lenses
PointingBetter than 1′ all-sky; absolute encoders with ≈ 4″ resolution
Dome15 m diameter, third floor; rotation and aperture driven by TPS, dome follows the telescope automatically

Operating limits

Altitude for EKARUS30°–88°. Below 30° dispersion degrades performance; above 80° take care, mechanical constraints near zenith.
Hard limitMinimum altitude 20°, set by software and limit switch; never drive the telescope there.
Humidity90%: keep the dome closed. 95%: close immediately (automatic alert).
Wind10 m/s: keep the dome closed.
